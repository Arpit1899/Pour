Hold a key. Say it.
It's typed.

Push-to-talk dictation for macOS. Hold ⌃Space, speak, let go, and the words are typed into whatever app you were using. Speech recognition and cleanup run on your Mac, so nothing you say leaves it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Hold ⌃Space

    Or double-tap it to keep listening hands-free. You can change the shortcut.

  2. 2

    Speak

    The bar glows amber while it listens. Talk the way you normally would.

  3. 3

    Let go

    The words are typed into the app you were using: mail, chat, notes, a browser.

What it does

Hold or press once

Hold ⌃Space while you talk. Double-tap it to keep listening after you let go, and press it again to finish. Or switch to press once to start and again to stop. Pick any shortcut you like.

A small bar on screen

It sits above the Dock, or on either side of the screen if you drag it there. Click it to start or stop a dictation.

Tidy text

With Apple Intelligence on, an on-device model adds punctuation and capitals and drops filler words, without rewording what you said. It can match the app: casual in chat, formal in mail, left alone in code editors and terminals.

Nowhere to paste?

If nothing you can type into is focused, the text waits in a card with a Copy button instead of being lost.

Your words, spelled right

Add names, acronyms and jargon to the Dictionary and the recogniser leans toward them. Pick the language and the microphone in Settings.

Insights

See your words per minute next to typing speed, the fixes cleanup and the Dictionary made, the typing time you saved, the kinds of app you dictate into, and a streak grid. Home shows a quick glance and your recent dictations to copy again.

Nothing leaves your Mac

Speech recognition uses Apple's on-device speech model, and cleanup uses the on-device Apple Intelligence model. Pour has no account and no server, and it sends no analytics. No audio or text leaves your Mac.

Recent dictations are saved in ~/Library/Application Support/Pour/history.json so they appear on the Home page. You can clear them from Home at any time. Insights keeps daily counts next to them in stats.json, never the text itself.

Install

First open

  1. Open Pour.dmg and drag Pour into Applications.
  2. Open Pour. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the message about Pour. Confirm with your password.

Pour isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to confirm once. Right-clicking and choosing Open no longer skips this check on recent macOS.

Prefer Terminal?

This removes the download flag so Pour opens normally:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Pour.app

Permissions

The first time Pour opens, a short welcome walks you through two permissions, the shortcut, and a first try:

  • Microphone and Speech Recognition, to hear you.
  • Accessibility, to type into the app you're using. Without it, Pour copies the text to the clipboard and you paste it with ⌘V. After you allow it, macOS may ask to quit and reopen Pour.

After you install a newer version, macOS may stop letting Pour type. Remove Pour from the Accessibility list in System Settings and add it again.

Requirements

  • macOS 26 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.
  • Apple Intelligence turned on, for the cleanup feature only. Dictation works without it.
  • A first-time download. The first time you dictate in a language, macOS downloads its speech model, which can take a minute.
